How it works

Where the number comes from

$5  ×  20.0 decimal  =  $100   (about +1900)
  • No single NFL prop pays 20-to-1 without being a bet you shouldn't want, so the board builds parlays — three to six legs.
  • The model doesn't guess at players. It reads the book's own lines, strips the margin out, and inverts them into projections. A line already knows about the injury report and the weather.
  • The edge comes from lines disagreeing with each other: a receiver's catch line and his yardage line imply different volume, and one of them is wrong. That disagreement is bounded — the model may move a projection 15% off its posted line, no further.
  • Probabilities are shaded down for leg count while the model is unproven, because errors compound: a model 10% optimistic per leg is 60% optimistic on a five-leg ticket.
  • A negative expected value is reported, not hidden. Most parlays are bad bets and the honest board says so; these are the least bad ones that clear $100.

Why two lineups

Floor versus ceiling

  • Double-up. Roughly the top half doubles up, and everyone above the cut wins the same. Upside past the cut pays nothing, so the build maximises the floor: no quarterback stack, at most two players per team, nothing that ties the whole lineup to one game going well.
  • Tournament. The prizes sit at the very top, so a good score is worthless and only a winning one counts. The build maximises the ceiling through correlation — a quarterback with his own receivers, plus an opponent to bring the game back — and pays a premium for low-owned players, because a lineup the field also built splits its prize with the field.
  • A lineup's floor and ceiling are the spread of the lineup's total, not the sum of nine players' individual bests. Uncorrelated players cancel each other out toward the average; stacked players don't, which is the arithmetic reason stacking wins tournaments.
  • Ownership is modelled, not scraped — the field chases points per dollar, so projected value predicts it well enough to tell chalk from leverage.
